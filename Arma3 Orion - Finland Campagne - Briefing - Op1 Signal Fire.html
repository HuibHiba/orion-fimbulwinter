OPERATION SIGNAL FIRE

SITUATION

Six hours ago, elements of the Russian 44th Army Corps crossed into Southern Karelia. Ahola — a border village of a few hundred people, sitting on ground Finland has lost and retaken twice before — is directly in their path. Article 5 has been invoked. It is also, at this hour, still a committee. Ahola does not have until the committee finishes talking.

The 44th's main body has not yet arrived in strength. Forward observer teams are already positioned to call for fire: one on high ground with a laser designator, a second at a radio post along the tree line north of the village. Until they are destroyed, every FDF position and every road out of Ahola is a target grid waiting to be read out loud.

A smaller threat sits closer to the village: a command post holding mortar stock earmarked for Ahola. If that shipment fires as planned, the evacuation route stops being a road and starts being a kill zone.

The FDF garrison is mostly reservists, called back for the first time since their service ended. They are expected to hold long enough to get their people out, not to win the position outright. Chernov wants a fast, visible win before Western attention catches up. That makes him impatient. Impatient enemies leave gaps. Orion is here to open them.

Orion International has been contracted directly, outside FDF command, to strip the 44th of its eyes and its opening barrage before the evacuation starts moving.

TASKING

MISSION

FIELD COMMAND DIRECTIVE

Orion elements will locate and destroy the Russian forward observer positions overlooking Ahola — the laser-designator team on the high point and the radio post along the tree line — and neutralise the command post holding the mortar stock earmarked for the village.

Once Ahola's evacuation begins, Orion will escort the FDF column from the village rally point to the egress road and conduct a fighting withdrawal to keep that road open until the column is clear.

ORDER OF BATTLE

EXECUTION

PHASE 1 / 4

Blind the Eyes

Locate and destroy the two confirmed forward observer positions: the laser-designator team on the high point, and the radio post along the tree line north of the village. Neither position is to be left active. A fire mission called in later is a direct result of a target missed here.

PHASE 2 / 4

Silence the Guns

Locate the small command post holding the mortar stock earmarked for Ahola. Intercept the fire plan if possible, then destroy the munitions on site. This shipment exists to hit the evacuation route specifically.

PHASE 3 / 4

Move the Column

Once Phase 1 and 2 objectives are down, escort the FDF evacuation column from the village rally point through Ahola to the designated egress road. The column is reservists and civilians, not combat troops. Orion provides the combat power.

PHASE 4 / 4

Fighting Withdrawal

Hold the egress road open as Russian reconnaissance elements and a light QRF move to intercept. This is a moving defence, not a static line. Expect to give ground deliberately rather than hold a fixed position past the point it costs the column.

Ground commanders retain discretionary authority over internal task allocation and approach across all phases.

FIELD RESTRICTIONS

RULES OF ENGAGEMENT

ROE // EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY
  • Any 44th Army Corps element observed, in contact, or confirmed at a designated objective is hostile. FDF reservists and Ahola civilians are not combatants, regardless of irregular equipment or bearing.
  • Lethal force is authorised against confirmed hostile positions without further clearance. This is a direct-action and defensive operation, not a restrained security posture.
  • A journalist, Nieminen — freelance, persistent — is known to be working the region. Orion is not present here officially. Do not get photographed.
  • Self-defence is authorised at all times without prior command approval.

TACTICAL OVERVIEW

The supply column route — Peltola, south of Ahola

This raid runs on the open farm road through Peltola, well south of Ahola. FDF hideout start point isn't marked — that changes depending on where Op 3 actually ended for your squad. Adjust markers once Eden placement is locked in.

OP 4

PAPER TIGER

Resupply raid. Intercept a Russian supply movement passing through Peltola's open farmland and take enough material back to run an operation again.

CONTINUITY — OP 3 → OP 4

After the marsh, Orion has almost nothing left. You start this op from an FDF hideout with exactly the loadout that survived Op 3 on your body — plus one small fixed emergency issue: a radio, bandages, one anti-tank round for whoever needs it most.

PHASE 1 / 5

Watch the roads

From a hilltop, scout the main road, a detour past a sawmill, and a roadworks site where the column is forced to slow down.

PHASE 2 / 5

Confirm the manifest

Raid a forward traffic post at a bridge to confirm the route and the order of the vehicles — which one carries ammunition, medical, anti-tank, or comms.

SIGINT — TRAFFIC POST RADIO, SHORT-RANGE, TIME UNKNOWN
"...confirm order: fuel truck lead, ammunition second, medical third, escort trails—"
"—copy, escort holds at the roadworks until the column clears the bridge—"
[POST SILENCED — manifest and vehicle order confirmed]
PHASE 3 / 5

The roadworks ambush

A short ambush at the roadworks: kill mobility, push the escort off, keep the supply trucks intact. Wrecked cargo doesn't feed the next operation.

PHASE 4 / 5

The sawmill

The raid widens to the sawmill, where a spare vehicle and stored crates are waiting. Secure the loading area, search the workshop, and prioritise which category of supply actually makes it out.

PHASE 5 / 5

Split the load

Divide the haul across two FDF vehicles and break for the forest route or the bridge before the armoured QRF can seal both.

COMBAT CLIMAX

Two fronts, one convoy

The motorised QRF splits the raid in two — armour down the main road, infantry through the sawmill. Orion has to hold the loot, a vehicle, and the exit route at once; one team pins the bridge while the rest fights through the last crates under fire.

Not a convoy-destroy op — the tactical win is worthless if Orion grabs the wrong crates or shoots up everything it was trying to save. You're literally rebuilding what Op 5 needs to happen.

WHY THIS ISN'T A NEW IDEA

Living off a beaten enemy's supply line

Finnish troops did exactly this in the Winter War — after a motti encirclement, half the point was the captured matériel, not the body count. Skis, weapons and rations taken off a broken Soviet column kept understrength Finnish units fighting through a winter their own logistics couldn't have sustained alone. Peltola's open farmland is the wrong place for a stand-up fight and the right place for a column to be forced to slow down — which is the entire tactical premise here: Orion isn't strong enough to win a fair fight yet, so it doesn't offer one. It picks the ground, picks the moment, and only wants what it can carry.

INTEL

Corporate & intercepted documents

ORION INTERNATIONAL — LOGISTICS NOTE · RE: RESUPPLY VIABILITY

Corporate isn't sending a resupply flight. That's not policy, that's arithmetic — a single air drop into a contested sector costs more in exposure than Zone-21 is currently worth on the books. You take what's on the ground, or you run Op 5 short.

Priorities, in order Corporate actually cares about them: comms gear (keeps you tasked without a courier run), medical (keeps the roster full), ammunition and anti-tank (keeps you alive long enough to matter). Read that order as an opinion, not an instruction.

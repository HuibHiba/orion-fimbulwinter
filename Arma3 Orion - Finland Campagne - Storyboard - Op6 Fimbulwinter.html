THREE ENDINGS

Which one plays depends on the whole campaign

IF THE CAMPAIGN WENT BADLY FOR ORION

FIMBULWINTER: WHITEOUT

ZONE-21 STATUS → OCCUPIED

Chernov never lost the initiative, and drives unopposed toward the FDF command post. The heaviest, most desperate defence of the campaign — a small specops raid first, against an artillery observer and an EW jammer, then a siege of your own base with a breakout through collapsing buildings and a tunnel corridor. An AC-130J and Fire Support Plus provide emergency cover; a Su-35 pushes the FA-18 out of the sky.

Final image: the team looks back from a ridge — the base burning, sirens in the snow. Odin comes over the net to say this was only the beginning.
IF THE CAMPAIGN WAS AN EVEN FIGHT

FIMBULWINTER: THE LAST CROSSING

ZONE-21 STATUS → INDEPENDENT, UNDER PRESSURE

Orion has slowed Chernov again and again without ever truly pinning him down. A Russian armoured column breaks toward the river town of Virtasilta; Orion holds an evacuation route open and seizes control of the bridge demolition charges. Street fighting between civilians, FDF, mechanised infantry and emergency services — the charges go off just after the last evacuation convoy clears.

Final image: the bridge comes down with part of the column still on it. Russia starts a pontoon crossing further down the line before the smoke clears.
IF ORION CAME OUT AHEAD

FIMBULWINTER: SKYFALL

ZONE-21 STATUS → ALLIED, STABILISING

Chernov's own deadline has become his problem. Orion has bought enough room, intelligence and disruption to hit the mobile EW command post directly and bring Finnish air support back into the sky. An FA-18 and a Su-35 duel overhead; once it's decided, Orion marks the crossing for an AC-130J fire mission.

Final image: the AC-130 circles through snow and smoke, the bridge zone burns, buildings collapse — and new Russian lights are already visible on the far bank.

No ending is a clean win or a clean loss. That's on purpose — this campaign doesn't end, it just stops being Orion's turn.

WHY THIS ISN'T A NEW IDEA

Blown bridges buy time, not victory

Every one of these three endings is a variation on the same old truth: destroying a river crossing never wins a war, it only rents time. German engineers dropped bridges across the whole Eastern Front in 1943-45 and the Red Army crossed anyway, days later, on pontoons, at a cost nobody on the defending side found comforting. The Last Crossing plays that exact trade — a won tactical moment bought with a permanent strategic problem, since Russia starts the pontoon crossing before the smoke clears. Whiteout and Skyfall are the same coin flipped: a desperate siege breakout borrows from encircled defenses like Bastogne, while Skyfall's air-superiority swing is the oldest lesson in this whole campaign — whoever owns the sky decides how the next battle starts, not how this one ends.

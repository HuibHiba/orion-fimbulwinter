OP 3

ASHES AND INK

The orders you took at the relay post show Russia is processing evidence, prisoners and local "suspects" across several sites around Ahola. Not one compound to raid — a hunt across the whole sector.

CONTINUITY — OP 1

How Ahola went matters here. Whoever got out, and however much evidence Russia found against Orion, shapes who you find — and who's still willing to help you — at each of these sites.

PHASE 1 / 4

Town hall

Raid a lightly guarded administrative building for transport lists and names. Paperwork first — it tells you where the people actually went.

PHASE 2 / 4

Woodcutters' crossing

Intercept a prisoner transport at a forest crossroads. Free the Finnish liaison on board — Aino Lehto — and take her knowledge of the roads with you.

PHASE 3 / 4

Forest depot

The documents lead here — an interrogation site buried in the trees. Destroy the archive server, and pull whatever hard military evidence you can carry out with you.

PHASE 4 / 4

Exfil

Load out by vehicle down a long forest route. It's the longest drive of the campaign so far — and the one time Corporate can't promise you a clean one.

COMBAT CLIMAX

The depot, both directions

The depot turns into close-quarters combat as reinforcements arrive from the road and the treeline at once. Choice under fire: spend the time you have left on the local dossiers and the suspects still inside, or on the military evidence and the liaison.

SCRIPTED OUTCOME

The marsh

This op ends badly on purpose. A Russian QRF cuts the exfil route, destroys the vehicle, and forces the team into the marsh on foot. Only what's physically carried on your body, vest and pack survives to Op 4 — vehicle stock, spare weapons, extra medical crates and any loot you didn't strap to yourself is gone.

Three compact fights connected by movement instead of one location to clear — this op uses the whole map instead of a single compound.

INTEL

Corporate & intercepted documents

ORION INTERNATIONAL — TARGETING NOTE · RE: EVIDENCE CHAIN, AHOLA SECTOR

Sorokina's desk wants a paper trail. That's a weakness, not a formality — it means the evidence physically exists in three places you can reach before it's photographed, filed and made permanent. Every hour you wait, more of it turns from "recoverable" into "published."

Command flags this as a soft priority, not a hard one. Getting the liaison out alive is worth more to the relationship with Helsinki than any single document. Corporate's math and Helsinki's math don't agree here — you'll have to pick.

INTERCEPTED
44TH CORPS — INFO-OPS DESK, TRANSFER MANIFEST (INTERCEPTED)

Detainees and materials ex-Ahola to be consolidated at forward processing site prior to transfer to ████████. Priority handling: documentation intended for narrative use (Sorokina, M., authorising). Liaison personnel to be held separately pending interrogation.

Site security: light garrison, reinforced from main road on alert. Do not assume the depot is undefended once contact begins.

"Rights groups ask what happened to detainees held near Ahola"

Human rights organisations say they have lost contact with sources describing a detention and processing site near the contested village of Ahola. Russian officials did not respond to requests for comment. Finnish officials would not confirm the site's existence, location, or status.

A Corps administrative facility near Ahola was destroyed in what officials describe as a "terrorist attack" by Western-backed forces. No mention is made of the facility's function. An unnamed liaison to Finnish forces is reported missing, presumed captured by the attackers.

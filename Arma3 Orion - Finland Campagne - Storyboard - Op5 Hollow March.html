TACTICAL OVERVIEW

Rautavaara Garrison — deep in Russian-held ground, far east of Ahola

Biggest footprint of the campaign so far, and the furthest from Ahola — this is well inside Russian-held territory. Adjust markers once Eden placement is locked in.

Rautavaara used to be an FDF reserve training garrison before the crossing — flat ground, existing hardstand and rail spur, which is exactly why the 44th Corps requisitioned it for its own logistics hub. Orion is hitting a Finnish base that's currently flying the wrong flag.

OP 5

HOLLOW MARCH

Use the salvage from Op 4 to convince Russia that Orion and the FDF are staging a major counter-offensive, while an assault element quietly blinds and deafens Rautavaara Garrison — the 44th Corps' logistics hub — behind it.

PHASE 1 / 4

Outer ring

Take down an observation post and an air-defence/alarm position on the hub's outer ring.

PHASE 2 / 4

Three convincing lies

Set up believable preparations at three separate points — captured ammunition, decoy vehicles, radio traffic, mines. Everything Op 4 salvaged becomes part of the story you're telling.

SIGINT — RUSSIAN RECON NET, PARTIAL, TIME UNKNOWN
"...counting vehicles at the tree line, more than expected, request confirmation on FDF strength in sector—"
"—command advises caution, possible large-scale buildup, hold reserve at readiness—"
[TRAFFIC CONTINUES — deception reads as credible, see hub approach]
PHASE 3 / 4

Into the hub

Infiltrate the edge of the hub itself. Take out the command post and its link to the reserve unit.

PHASE 4 / 4

Hold the lie

Keep the deception credible while Russian troops redeploy toward the wrong sector — then break contact at the hub's edge before it collapses on you.

COMBAT CLIMAX

The trap closes both ways

The moment the hub goes blind and deaf, the Russian reserve turns back — and the outer ring becomes a real battlefield. Orion has to keep all three deception points alive, keep the hub's link to command dead, and hold a fire-support/demolition point at the rail yard. A motorised group comes in off the road while hub security presses from the other side — and you get one shot at the fuel and rail zone, catching the returning reserve inside its own supply corridor. Then a breakout through smoke, burning vehicles and the collapsed outer ring.

The attack is a means, not the goal — winning means the enemy reserve drives into the wrong place at the wrong time, with a real trap waiting when it gets there. The supply you fought for in Op 4 is visibly part of the lie.

WHY THIS ISN'T A NEW IDEA

Making the enemy defeat himself

This is the same logic as Operation Fortitude before D-Day — not "hide the real attack" but "build a more convincing fake one out of scraps." Fortitude used dummy tanks, fake radio traffic and a phantom army group to keep German reserves pinned at Calais for weeks after the real landings started at Normandy. Hollow March runs the same play at operational scale: three points of "evidence" built from Op 4's actual salvage, real radio discipline broken on purpose, and a commander under enough deadline pressure to want to believe it. Deception doesn't need to fool Chernov forever. It only needs to fool him for the six hours the hub is dark.

INTEL

Corporate & intercepted documents

ORION INTERNATIONAL — TARGETING NOTE · RE: HUB DECEPTION, CHERNOV'S DEADLINE

Chernov is now two weeks into a war he told his own command would take days. That clock is worth more to Orion than any single objective in this sector — a commander watching a deadline slip is a commander who believes bad news before he checks it.

This only works once. If the hub survives with its reserve link intact, Chernov stops trusting sector reports entirely, and every future deception in this campaign gets harder, not easier.

T+14 DAYS — SOMETHING MOVED
REUTERS — WIRE / 09:15 GMT

"Satellite imagery shows Russian reserve repositioning in South Karelia, cause unclear"

Commercial satellite imagery reviewed by open-source analysts shows a Russian reserve formation repositioning twice within 48 hours near the Karelian front, an unusual pattern analysts describe as "consistent with responding to false or contradictory intelligence." Neither Russian nor Finnish officials have commented.

"Плановая переброска резерва прошла успешно"

The Ministry of Defence describes reserve repositioning near the front as "planned and successful," part of what it calls routine operational flexibility. Military bloggers online have questioned the timeline, noting the movement appears to have been reversed within a day of being ordered.

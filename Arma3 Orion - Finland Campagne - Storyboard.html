WORLD BRIEFING

Everything you need to know before Ahola

ORION INTERNATIONAL

A private army older than this war

Founded during the Fall of Europe — a cascade of political and economic crises that left the continent's collective security structures too slow, too divided, to act as one. Individual states were on their own. Into that vacuum grew corporations like Orion: security contractors that became power brokers.

"Founded during the Fall of Europe, Orion International emerged as a visionary proxy corporation led by CEO Lucien Stroud. Our mission: to defend against chaos where standard governments falter. Initially a small unit, we've rapidly expanded, becoming the world's foremost military corporation, surpassing even most superpowers in active service member count and redefining the private sector around the globe."

What that makes you: not a national army, not a peacekeeping force. Orion answers to contracts and to Corporate's own interests, at the same time, on purpose.

"Be a part of the world's first private army, not held back by financial or diplomatic ties, and carry out Corporate's agenda. For better or for worse…"
THE BREAKPOINT

Finland is already NATO. So why call a PMC?

Finland joined NATO in 2023, for real, in our world too — that part of the timeline hasn't changed. In Fimbulwinter, Helsinki does exactly what you'd expect: it invokes Article 5, through the front door, the same day the 44th Army Corps crosses the line.

The problem is speed. Article 5 is a process — consensus, escalation ladders, allied timelines. As part of the same "Fall of Europe" that grew Corporate in the first place, NATO's collective decision-making has quietly become unreliable: membership stands, but nobody can promise it moves fast enough. Finland doesn't wait to find out. It runs the official channel and a second, deniable one at the same time — Corporate, on the ground, hours after the first intelligence, weeks before any alliance response could plausibly land.

You are the second channel. That's the whole reason this campaign exists.

WHY THIS WAR IS REAL, NOT GENERIC

Karelia has done this before

Winter War, 1939-40. Nearly half a million Soviet troops crossed into Finland. Outnumbered five to one, Finnish troops used the terrain itself as a weapon — the motti tactic: encircle and cut off Soviet columns on the narrow forest roads, sometimes by felling trees across them. Soviet armour got stuck. Finnish troops on skis didn't. It took a massive Soviet artillery push in February 1940 to finally break the line.

Karelia, 1944. Finland ceded roughly 40,000 km² of this exact ground after the follow-up war — the Karelian Isthmus, Ladoga Karelia, Border Karelia — and evacuated 420,000 people. Ahola sits in Finnish South Karelia: land the country has already lost once, and already taken back once. That history is why the villagers in this campaign react the way they do.

Finnish total defense. Shelter capacity for over 80% of the population, conscription plus a reserve system that can mobilize roughly 700,000 people — close to 15% of the country — in an emergency. That's why Ahola has a shelter and an organized evacuation drill, and why the FDF column in Op 1 is built from reservists, not professional soldiers.

2023 to now. NATO accession in 2023 doubled Russia's practical land border with the alliance overnight. Finland built 200km of border fencing. Russia answered by standing up a new formation in the Republic of Karelia around the Petrozavodsk garrison — the real, still-being-built 44th Army Corps. In our fiction, that build-up is deliberately fast-forwarded to fully operational. That's the one honest embellishment in this whole briefing — everything above it happened, or is happening, for real.

THE CONTRACT

What you're actually here for

Officially: Orion runs deniable specops the FDF can't run itself before a formal declaration of war — Finland keeps plausible deniability while buying real capability.

Unofficially: Corporate sees a permanent foothold on NATO's northern flank as worth more than the invoice. This is transactional, not heroic. Whatever happens to Ahola, Corporate already won the day the contract was signed.

NEWS

How the world sees it (in-fiction) — the week of Signal Fire

T-3 DAYS — BEFORE THE CROSSING
YLE NEWS — NATIONAL DESK

"Finland raises readiness along eastern border amid Russian troop movements"

The Finnish Defence Forces have quietly raised readiness levels in South Karelia, officials confirmed Tuesday, citing "unusual concentrations" tied to Russia's 44th Army Corps near Petrozavodsk. The Ministry of Defence declined to call the movements a threat, pointing to the corps' ongoing, unfinished build-up. Analysts note the timing follows closely on NATO's Cold Response exercises. "We watch. We do not panic," one MoD spokesperson said. "We have done this before."

T-0 — THE MORNING OF THE CROSSING
KARJALAN SANOMAT — SOUTH KARELIA EDITION

"Unrest at border village Ahola, authorities urge calm"

Residents of the border village of Ahola report heavy blasts in the early morning. The Finnish Defence Forces confirm "heightened readiness" in the region but stop short of calling it a formal attack. Local elder Väinö Ahola: "We've been through this before. We know what it means when the roads close."

TASS — ГРАНИЧНАЯ ЗОНА / BORDER ZONE

"Западные наёмники обстреляли мирное население у финской границы"

Sources within the 44th Army Corps report that unidentified armed units — presumed Western private contractors — are responsible for explosions near Ahola alleged to have caused civilian casualties. Moscow calls it "a direct provocation that justifies retaliation."

REUTERS — WIRE / 06:41 GMT

"Finland invokes Article 5 as Russian forces cross border near Ahola"

Finland formally invoked NATO's Article 5 collective-defence clause on Thursday after confirming Russian units crossed into South Karelia, alliance officials said, though a joint response has yet to be agreed. Unconfirmed local reports describe armed personnel operating alongside Finnish reservists near Ahola who "did not match any known FDF unit" — a detail NATO officials declined to comment on. "This is moving faster than our process," one diplomat said on condition of anonymity.
